## 1. Objective and mid-November feasibility (corrected)

**Goal:** launch Mogra-branded culinary herbs and spices on Amazon US, targeting buyable inventory by mid-November and momentum into December.

### Published holiday arrival guidance — what it does and does not mean

Amazon's published 2026 Black Friday / Cyber Monday readiness dates:

| Channel | Published arrival date |
|---|---|
| AWD | **Oct 14, 2026** |
| FBA (minimal shipment splits) | **Oct 21, 2026** |
| FBA (Amazon-optimized splits) | **Oct 28, 2026** |

**Corrected interpretation.** These dates are **published holiday arrival guidance for event readiness**. They establish **readiness risk** for a later arrival — they do **not** prove that a shipment arriving after those dates cannot become buyable. Amazon accepts inbound outside the peak window; what the dates govern is **guaranteed Prime-badge / event readiness**, not whether inventory can be received and sold.

**Therefore:** a Nov 15 launch carries **high risk of missing Black Friday/Cyber Monday event readiness**, and **cannot be guaranteed** to be event-ready. It is not, however, automatically impossible to sell in December.

### Four separate things — do not conflate them

| Stage | What it means | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Published holiday arrival guidance** | Amazon's stated event-readiness dates (Oct 14/21/28) | **VERIFIED** |
| **2. Supplier production timing** | How long a supplier takes to produce | **UNKNOWN** — no supplier has quoted a lead time |
| **3. Delivery to Amazon** | Shipment physically arrives at a fulfilment centre | **UNKNOWN** |
| **4. Receiving and buyable inventory** | Check-in completes; the ASIN is buyable | **UNKNOWN** — always later than delivery |
| **Actual event / program eligibility** | Whether the listing qualifies for the event or Prime badge | **UNRESOLVED** |

**No guarantee is made** for November 15, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, or December availability.

### Pricing-lines reconciliation (54 → 15)

An earlier report cited **54 pricing lines**. That figure was produced by a **malformed intermediate CSV** whose unquoted commas inside address and notes fields split rows into extra fields, inflating a naive count. When the table was rebuilt with correct CSV quoting, the genuine count is **15** — because **most suppliers publish no price at all**, so they generate no pricing line. The 54 figure is **withdrawn**.

**Pricing lines (15) ≠ economics lines (17).** They measure different things:
- **Pricing lines** = one row per actual *price or explicit quote-required position* on a supplier offer (`pricing_comparison.csv`, 15).
- **Economics lines** = one row per *cost or price component in a modelled SKU* — many of which are UNKNOWN or FORMULA_ONLY (`economics_scenarios.csv`, 17) plus `hypothetical_economics.csv` (26).

They are not the same table and must not be conflated.
