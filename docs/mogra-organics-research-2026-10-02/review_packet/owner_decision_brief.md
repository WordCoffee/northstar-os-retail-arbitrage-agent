**Why it matters:** every RFQ, contract, invoice and customs entry names a legal entity. An unverified spelling cannot appear on a supplier-facing document.
**Options:** confirm **T2Holdings LLC** exact registered spelling · or designate a different entity.
**Tradeoff:** no research can substitute for owner records.

**If deferred:** RFQs stay unsendable; customs entry cannot be filed.

### D2 — Initial product and form
**Why it matters:** determines the supplier shortlist, certification scope and packaging.
**Options:** black pepper (whole + ground) · turmeric (powder) · both · add cinnamon/herbs · add a salt-containing seasoning blend.
**Evidence-backed tradeoff:** **black pepper (whole + ground) is the strongest supported start** — Pearl's certification explicitly covers both forms (verified in the USDA OID). **Turmeric powder carries an open evidence gap** (see D9/P1). Adding herbs is efficient *if* using Pearl, whose certificate covers many herbs.
**Recommendation:** start with black pepper whole + ground as the first verified SKU.
**If deferred:** quotes remain generic, not comparable.

### D3 — Organic claim / labelling pathway
**Why it matters:** drives certification cost, label design and minimum supplier requirements.
**Options:** certified "100% organic" · certified "organic" · "made with organic [ingredient]" · ingredient-only organic identification · no organic claim.
**Evidence-backed tradeoff:** single-ingredient spices can carry "organic" **if the finished pack is certified**; multi-ingredient blends may only support "made with organic". Removing the seal does **not** necessarily remove certification obligations.
**Recommendation:** decide **per SKU**, not brand-wide.
**If deferred:** label design cannot start; certifier review cannot begin.

### D5 — Preferred sourcing route
**Why:** determines which suppliers to engage first.
**Options:** **A** overseas finished/private-label · **B** imported bulk + US processing/packing · **C** US turnkey.
**Evidence-backed tradeoff:**

| Route | Strength | Weakness | Evidence state |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | One verified supplier covers pepper + herbs; lowest handling steps | Concentration on one overseas entity; finished-pack organic status unconfirmed | Pearl organic **verified**; packing **claimed** |
| **B** | Bulk flexibility; US co-packing control | Two entities; import + organic-importer obligation; **no broker/duty position yet** | Bulk organic verified for Pearl; US co-packer organic **unverified** |
| **C** | No import; simplest chain | US organic ingredient cost likely higher (unproven); thin domestic turmeric supply | Ingredient + co-packer organic **unverified** |

**Recommendation:** investigate **A and B in parallel**; hold **C** as fallback. **No route is declared cheapest** — that requires comparable quotes plus import assumptions.
**If deferred:** no quotes worth collecting at scale.

## Decisions needed BEFORE IMPORTING / PRODUCTION

### D8 — Importer and FSVP posture
**Why:** determines duty liability, FSVP and organic-importer obligations.
**Options:** Mogra imports directly · buy from a US distributor who already imported.
**Tradeoff:** direct import adds **organic-importer** and **FSVP** obligations and fixed cost, but improves control and margin.
**Recommendation:** confirm before any route A/B import.
**If deferred:** import cost model is invalid.

### D9 — Applicable certification arrangement
**Why:** determines whether Mogra must certify its **own** handling/import activity.
**Options:** certify as importer/handler/repacker · avoid by buying finished from a certified packer.
**Key point:** **an organic input does not certify the Mogra pack.** Certifying is a **process, not a purchase**.
**Recommendation:** obtain a certifier opinion before committing to route B.
**If deferred:** route B economics and timeline are invalid.
