## 2. The four ways low-sodium rubs fail, and what we did about each

This is the whole competitive strategy. Whatever the listing you sent does, a low-sodium
BBQ rub almost always dies on one of these four.

| Failure | Why it happens | BARKSIDE answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Tastes flat** | Salt carries flavour and suppresses bitterness. Cut it 60% and the blend reads hollow. | Umami stack: mushroom 5%, tomato 3%, coffee 4%, cocoa 1.5%, plus 0.8% citric acid for lift. Salt drops, glutamate and acid take over the job. |
| **No bark** | Sugar burns before bark sets; wet surfaces steam instead of brown. | Turmeric + paprika + coffee + cocoa build colour chemically. Copper holds sugar at **zero** so it can run 275–325 °F without scorching. Pack copy says pat dry, no oil, 45 min rest. |
| **Clumps into a brick** | Low-salt blends are hygroscopic; paprika and turmeric pull water. | 1.5–2% rice bran, foil-lined pouch, moisture ≤8%, aw ≤0.60 on the COA. |
| **One pouch does half a brisket** | 80 g is small. At 12 g/lb it covers 6.7 lb. | Say it out loud on the pouch: *"Seasons one 6–7 lb pork butt or one brisket flat."* Undersell the yield, never let the buyer discover it. |

## 4. Why turmeric is a brand asset, not an ingredient

Everyone else treats turmeric as a health additive and buries it at 1%. At 4.5–5% it
does something no competitor can copy without rebranding: it makes **bark colour the
product architecture**.

- Copper bark on beef and pork — the exact visual the brisket buyer is chasing.
- Gold bark on pork and chicken — a Carolina look without a mustard slather.
- The colour survives the cook because curcumin is heat-stable, unlike paprika's
  carotenoids which fade over four hours at 250 °F.
- It also ties directly to the supply you already have: Pearl Group's organic turmeric
  is the one verified-organic line in the existing research, with curcumin and lead
  specs available per lot.

**Cost of the idea:** turmeric stains. Hands, boards, plastic, countertops. Put
*"Turmeric will stain — gloves recommended"* on the back panel. It reads as
professional, and it pre-empts the one-star review.

## 5. Packaging — 80 g

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Format | Stand-up pouch, resealable zip, 80 g net |
| Barrier | Foil-lined or metallised PET/PE — **opaque**, no window |
| Why opaque | Turmeric and paprika colour degrades in light. This is a functional requirement, not a design choice. |
| Finish | Matte, kraft-look or deep charcoal; colour band per SKU |
| Front | Brand mark, blend name, colour chip, salt %, "80 g / 2.8 oz" |
| Back | 3-line method, yield statement, allergens, turmeric stain note, lot + best-before |
| Fill tolerance | ±0.5 g, gross weight check per 30 pouches |
| Coding | Lot + best-before inkjet on seal; no sticker |

Opaque packaging is also the cheapest way to beat a competitor who uses a clear window:
their paprika goes brown on the shelf in six weeks and their reviews say so.
