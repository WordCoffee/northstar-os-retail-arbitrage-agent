# BARKSIDE — claims and labelling

**Read the market fork first. It is the one thing that can kill the whole positioning.**

---

## 1. The fork: "low sodium" is a US claim only

| Market | Basis of a nutrition claim | Does "low sodium" work? |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | Per serving (RACC). "Low sodium" = ≤ 140 mg per serving. | **Yes.** 29–34 mg per 1/4 tsp. |
| **UK / EU** | Per **100 g** (Reg. 1924/2006). "Low salt" = ≤ 0.12 g salt per 100 g. | **No. Impossible.** Our blends are 11–13 g salt per 100 g — ~100× the threshold. No reformulation gets there: a rub with 0.12 g salt per 100 g is not a BBQ rub. |
| **Canada** | Per stated serving | Likely yes; verify against CFIA Table of Nutrient Content Claims before print. |

**If the listed competitor is UK/EU (80 g pouches suggest it might be), they are not
legally able to say "low sodium" either.** They are almost certainly saying "reduced
salt" or leading with a number. That is your opening, and it means the UK play is:

- Lead with the **factual number**: *"13 g salt per 100 g. Most BBQ rubs are 35."*
- "**Reduced salt**" is available (≥30% less than similar products, with the
  comparison stated) — we are 63–68% under. Substantiation file required.
- Never print "low salt", "low sodium", "salt-free" or "no added salt" on an EU/UK pack.

## 2. US claim set

| Claim | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **"Low Sodium"** | ✅ Available | ≤140 mg per RACC. We declare **1/4 tsp (0.6 g)** → 29–34 mg. Headroom 75–79%. |
| "1/4 tsp serving" | ✅ | Confirm the RACC against 21 CFR 101.12(b) — dry seasonings are 1/4 tsp, but confirm in writing with the label reviewer, not from memory. |
| "Reduced sodium" / "Less sodium" | ⚠️ Conditional | ≥25% less than a reference food, which must be named. We beat a 35%-salt rub by 63–68%, but you need a *specific* competitor's panel to name. Do not use until you have one. |
| "Salt-free" / "No salt added" | ❌ Never | False. Salt is 11–13% of the blend. |
| "No MSG added" | ⚠️ Avoid | True but it reads as a processed-food claim and invites "then why does it taste like something". |
| "Clean label" / "All natural" | ⚠️ Avoid | Undefined, unenforceable, and USDA/FTC will not defend it for you. |
| "Organic" | ⚠️ Conditional | See §4. |

### The claim to actually lead with

Not "low sodium." **"Salt is 13% of this blend. Most BBQ rubs are 35%."**

It is factual, verifiable from the panel, survives any challenge, and it does the
persuasion that a regulated claim cannot: it makes the buyer feel informed rather
than managed. Put it on the front of the pouch and in the first line of the bullet
points.

### Serving-size trap

At the house rate of 12 g/lb of meat, one pound of meat carries **518–609 mg sodium**
from the rub, and a 4 oz cooked portion carries roughly **130–150 mg**. That is real
and it is fine — but it means:

- **Do not** declare 1 tsp as the serving size. Copper lands at 138 mg against a
  140 mg limit. It passes on paper and fails the first time a lab result runs 2% high.
- Keep a "per 1 tsp" line in the *marketing* copy for transparency, never as the
  basis of the claim.
- Never write "low sodium" next to a recipe that uses a whole pouch on one butt.

## 3. Allergens

| | US (FALCPA + sesame) | UK/EU (Annex II) |
|---|---|---|
| COPPER | none of the 9 majors | **mustard, celery** — bold in the ingredient list |
| GOLD | none of the 9 majors | **mustard, celery** — bold |
| CRIMSON | none of the 9 majors | **none** |

Mustard and celery are Annex II allergens in the UK/EU and are **not** US major
allergens. The same blend therefore needs two different label texts. Do not print one
label for both markets.

Also required on EU/UK packs: "may contain" only if a genuine cross-contact risk is
assessed — do not add it defensively, it is a competitive liability on a free-from
positioning.

## 4. Organic — the window, and the catch

Salt is a non-agricultural ingredient and is **excluded** from the NOP percentage
calculation. So a blend that is 87% organic spice and 13% allowed salt can still
qualify for the "Organic" seal — it is not diluted by the salt.

**The catch is not the formula, it is the building.** If your operation blends or
repacks organic material, that handling activity needs its own certification. The
upstream supplier's certificate does not cover your blend room. This is the same
unresolved issue already logged in the Mogra research (`shortlist.md` §"Certification
dependency").

| Route | Organic % (salt excluded) | Seal available |
|---|---|---|
| All spices certified organic, blender certified handler | 100% of agricultural ingredients | **"Organic"** |
| ≥70% organic spices by weight | 70–95% | "Made with organic ingredients" — no seal |
| Any non-organic spice | <70% | No organic claim at all |

Cost delta is roughly **+35% on ingredient cost** (turmeric and pepper are the two
inputs where your verified supply already exists). That is $0.20–0.30 per pouch — cheap
for the seal, if the handler certification is already in place or cheap to add.

**Do not print the seal until the certificate names your blending operation.**

## 6. Nutrition panel basis (US)

Per declared serving of 1/4 tsp (0.6 g). Values from the formulation model — **replace
with a lab panel before print.**

| | COPPER | GOLD | CRIMSON |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Serving size | 1/4 tsp (0.6 g) | 1/4 tsp (0.6 g) | 1/4 tsp (0.6 g) |
| Servings per pouch | ~133 | ~133 | ~133 |
| Calories | ~1 | ~1 | ~1 |
| Total fat | 0 g | 0 g | 0 g |
| Sodium | **34 mg** | **32 mg** | **29 mg** |
| Total carb | <1 g | <1 g | <1 g |
| Protein | 0 g | 0 g | 0 g |
| Sodium %DV | 1% | 1% | 1% |

EU/UK declaration is per 100 g and will read: **salt 13.0 / 12.0 / 11.0 g**. Print it.
The number is the marketing.
