# BARKSIDE — formulation spec sheets

All percentages are **% w/w of the finished dry blend**. Batch columns are for a
100 kg pilot. Computed totals, sodium and cost: [`sodium_report.md`](sodium_report.md),
regenerate with `python docs/barkside-launch/sodium_model.py`.

**House application rate: 12 g per lb of meat (≈1.5 tbsp).** 80 g seasons ~6.7 lb —
one 6–7 lb pork butt, one brisket flat, or two racks plus a chicken. A full 12–14 lb
packer takes two pouches, and the pack copy says so.

---

## No.1 COPPER — brisket + pork butt

*Copper bark, no brine. Zero sugar.*

**Why it works.** Coarse black pepper at 23% is the Texan backbone and it also gives
the bark its physical texture — fine pepper packs flat, cracked pepper builds a
surface. Salt sits at 13% instead of the usual 35%, and the flavour that salt would
have carried is replaced by four umami sources (mushroom, tomato, coffee, cocoa) plus
0.8% citric acid to keep the finish from going muddy. **There is no sugar in this
blend on purpose:** sugar is what turns a 14-hour brisket into a bitter black shell
above 300 °F, and most low-sodium rubs add sugar to cover the missing salt.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| black pepper, coarse (6–8 mesh) | 23.0 | 18.4 | 23.0 |
| smoked paprika | 13.5 | 10.8 | 13.5 |
| sea salt, coarse | 13.0 | 10.4 | 13.0 |
| garlic, granulated | 8.5 | 6.8 | 8.5 |
| onion, granulated | 8.5 | 6.8 | 8.5 |
| mushroom powder (porcini/shiitake) | 5.0 | 4.0 | 5.0 |
| turmeric, ground | 4.5 | 3.6 | 4.5 |
| coffee, ground roasted | 4.0 | 3.2 | 4.0 |
| tomato powder | 3.0 | 2.4 | 3.0 |
| mustard, ground | 2.5 | 2.0 | 2.5 |
| celery seed, ground | 2.0 | 1.6 | 2.0 |
| coriander, ground | 2.0 | 1.6 | 2.0 |
| ancho chile, ground | 2.0 | 1.6 | 2.0 |
| rice bran | 2.0 | 1.6 | 2.0 |
| cocoa powder | 1.5 | 1.2 | 1.5 |

**Grind:** bimodal on purpose. Pepper cracked to 6–8 mesh; everything else through
20 mesh. The coarse fraction must survive the blender — check sieve retention on the
first batch, not the tenth.

| Application | Amount | Method | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Packer brisket** | 2 pouches | Pat dry, no oil, coat all sides | 45 min rest, then 250–275 °F |
| **Brisket flat** | 1 pouch | Same | 45 min |
| **Pork butt (6–7 lb)** | 1 pouch | Score fat cap, work into the cuts | 45 min, then 275 °F |
| **Beef short rib** | ½ pouch per 3 lb | Heavy coat | 30 min |
| **Dry-brine chicken thighs** | 2 tsp per lb | Skin on, under the skin | Overnight |

**Do not** wrap in foil before the stall. Turmeric and coffee want dry surface heat to
set the colour; foil at 165 °F is fine, foil at 150 °F gives a yellow-grey bark.

## No.2 GOLD — pulled pork + chicken

*Gold bark, tang on the back end.*

**Why it works.** This is the Carolina flavour (mustard + vinegar) built as a dry rub.
Mustard powder at 15% is the identity; 7% vinegar powder delivers the tang without
putting liquid on the surface, which is what kills bark on a basted shoulder. Only one
of the three blends carries sugar (8% coconut sugar) because pork and chicken want a
glaze and beef does not. Turmeric at 5% gives the gold — the same colour a mustard
slather produces, without the slather.

| **Roast potatoes / corn** | to taste | Toss in 1 tbsp oil first | — |

**Sugar warning on pack:** contains coconut sugar — keep direct heat under 350 °F or
the glaze scorches before the meat is done.

## No.3 CRIMSON — steak, chicken thighs, charred veg

*Red bark, bright finish.*

**Why it works.** Two real chiles (ancho for raisin-sweet depth, guajillo for clean
red) instead of one generic "chili powder" that is mostly salt and cumin. Lime powder
at 6% gives acid without wetting the surface — the reason most chile-lime rubs fail on
a grill is that they are applied as a wet paste. Cumin at 9% is high, and correct: it
is the note people identify as "steakhouse" without being able to name it. No turmeric,
so the red is honest.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ancho chile, ground | 17.0 | 13.6 | 17.0 |
| guajillo chile, ground | 11.0 | 8.8 | 11.0 |
| sea salt, coarse | 11.0 | 8.8 | 11.0 |
| garlic, granulated | 10.0 | 8.0 | 10.0 |
| cumin, ground | 9.0 | 7.2 | 9.0 |
| smoked paprika | 8.0 | 6.4 | 8.0 |
| onion, granulated | 8.0 | 6.4 | 8.0 |
| black pepper, ground | 7.0 | 5.6 | 7.0 |
| lime powder | 6.0 | 4.8 | 6.0 |
| coriander, ground | 5.0 | 4.0 | 5.0 |
| oregano, ground | 3.0 | 2.4 | 3.0 |
| citric acid | 1.5 | 1.2 | 1.5 |
| rice bran | 1.5 | 1.2 | 1.5 |

| chipotle, ground | 1.0 | 0.8 | 1.0 |

**Grind:** 30–40 mesh. Fine enough to bloom on contact with a hot surface, coarse
enough not to turn to paste with a little oil.

| Application | Amount | Method | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Steak (1" thick)** | 1 tsp per side | Oil lightly first — this one wants oil | 20 min, or 2 h dry-brine |
| **Skirt / flank** | 1 tbsp per lb | Heavier coat, hot and fast | 15 min |
| **Chicken thighs** | 2 tsp per lb | Skin on, direct heat | 20 min |
| **Charred veg / corn** | 1 tbsp per lb | Toss with oil | — |
| **Shrimp** | 1 tsp per lb | 10 min, no longer | — |

**Note:** this is the only blend with no mustard and no celery, so it is also the only
one that clears EU/UK allergen bolding cleanly.

## 8. Shared production spec

| Parameter | Spec |
|---|---|
| Blending | Ribbon or paddle blender, 8–10 min, no heat generation |
| Charge order | Salt + coarse pepper → fine powders → colours (paprika, turmeric) last |
| Environment | RH ≤ 45%, ≤ 25 °C, no direct sunlight on open powder |
| Metal control | Magnet + metal detector on finished pouch, 2.0 mm Fe / 2.5 mm non-Fe |
| Fill | Auger filler, 80 g ± 0.5 g, check-weigh every 30 pouches |
| Seal | Dye penetration or vacuum decay, per lot |
| Moisture | ≤ 8.0% finished blend |
| Water activity | ≤ 0.60 |
| Micro | TPC < 10⁴ cfu/g · Salmonella absent / 25 g · Listeria absent / 25 g · E. coli absent / g · yeast & mould < 10² cfu/g |
| Shelf life | 24 months unopened at ≤ 25 °C, away from light; 6 months after opening |
| Lot coding | Julian lot + best-before, inkjet on seal, no sticker |

**Anti-caking.** 1.5–2% rice bran is the clean-label route. If the supplier cannot
guarantee ≤ 8% moisture, the alternative is 1% silicon dioxide (E551) — it works
better and it reads worse on the label. Decide with a humidity-chamber test on the
pilot batch, not on opinion.
