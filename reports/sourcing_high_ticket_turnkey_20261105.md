## 0. THE FILTER YOU ACTUALLY ASKED FOR

"$15 net per unit" is a structural filter, not a preference. It excludes most of the
catalogue you have been looking at. At a 15% referral fee, FBA fulfilment and $0.20 prep:

> **net = 0.85 × retail − FBA − 0.20 − landed cost**

Solving for the maximum landed cost that still clears $15:

| Retail | FBA (est.) | Max landed cost | Cost ratio | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $30 | $4.50 | **$5.80** | 19% | effectively impossible |
| $40 | $5.50 | $13.30 | 33% | tight — needs a cheap, light product |
| $50 | $6.00 | $21.30 | 43% | workable |
| $60 | $6.50 | $29.30 | 49% | comfortable |
| $70 | $7.00 | $37.30 | 53% | comfortable |
| $80 | $7.50 | $45.30 | 57% | very comfortable |

**Two conclusions.**

1. **Below ~$40 retail, $15 net is not a sourcing problem — it is arithmetic that does not close.**
   You would need a 19% cost ratio, which no legitimate supplier offers on a branded good.
2. **The higher the ticket, the more cost ratio you can tolerate.** A $70 product at 50% cost
   ratio still nets $15+. A $30 product at 50% nets nothing. So stop optimising for cheap
   sourcing and start optimising for **price ceiling**.

## 1. THE COUNTERINTUITIVE FINDING: TRANSIT IS NOT THE BOTTLENECK

You asked for suppliers "closer than China" because you believe closer = faster. For
**turnkey stock goods it usually does not**, and it is worth being explicit about why.

| | China (stock/turnkey) | Mexico (custom) |
|---|---|---|
| Dispatch from order | 3–7 days | 4–8 weeks production |
| Transit to US | 7–14 days air | 3–7 days truck |
| **Total** | **2–3 weeks** | **5–9 weeks** |

A Chinese factory holding stock beats a Mexican factory making to order, because the
Chinese ecosystem is built for small-run turnkey and the Mexican one is built for
contract manufacturing at volume.

**Near-shoring wins on four things, none of which is raw speed:**

1. **Freight economics on heavy or bulky goods.** A 20 kg carton from China costs more to
   fly than the goods. From Mexico it is a truck.
2. **Reorder cadence.** If you restock every 3 weeks, a 3-day truck beats a 10-day air leg
   with customs.
3. **Tariff exposure.** USMCA at 0% versus Section 301 at 25%+ is a 25-point swing on COGS.
4. **Cash cycle.** No 30-day ocean float, no customs broker, smaller deposits.

**And one 2026 headwind you should know before committing:** Mexico imposed tariffs in
January 2026 on imported intermediate goods. Mexican factories that assemble from Chinese
components are getting more expensive, not less. Mexico works best for goods made from
**domestic inputs** — leather, cotton, clay, silver — not for assembly of imported parts.

\* ~85% of export-oriented Mexican manufacturing qualifies under USMCA (Tetakawi, 2026),
but you must hold a **certification of origin** — duty-free is not automatic.

**Ranking by what you actually want (speed to shelf):** USA → Mexico → Canada → China air →
Colombia/Peru → Vietnam/India/Turkey.

**Ranking by turnkey availability at low MOQ:** China → USA (narrow lanes) → India → Vietnam →
Mexico → everywhere else.

The tension is the whole answer: **the fastest country has the shallowest turnkey catalogue,
and the deepest turnkey catalogue is the slowest.**

## 5. WHAT TO ASK EVERY SUPPLIER (the five that decide it)

1. **"What is your landed cost to my door, DDP?"** — not FOB. FOB hides freight and duty.
2. **"Do you hold stock of this exact item today, or is it made to order?"** — this single
   question separates a 2-week lead from a 9-week lead.
3. **"What is your MOQ at my label, and what is the price at 3× MOQ?"** — reveals whether
   the price curve has a cliff.
4. **"Can you ship 50 units first, then 300?"** — tests whether they are a real small-run
   supplier or a broker.
5. **"Send the packing dimensions and carton weight."** — FBA cost is decided by this, and
   it is the number most likely to kill an otherwise good product.
