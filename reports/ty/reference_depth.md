## Why this is a scored gap, not housekeeping

The Seller-Tester's rubric is built from real packaging. A category with **one** reference is
one photograph's opinion: a single jar decides what "a food jar" means, and a brief that
looks nothing like it is judged wrong. `reference_coverage` exists for exactly this, and
before this unit three categories were thin — `ceramic_mug` 2, `skincare_bottle` 1,
`food_jar` 1.

## Honest limits

* **The headline I first wrote was wrong.** Row counts said every category had three; the
  coverage tool counts *usable after review*, and five categories are still at one or two.
  The corrected table above is the one to quote.

* **"Context" is not "product photograph".** Two of the five kept rows are retail-shelf or
  collage pictures: they support *what a category's packaging looks like*, not *how a product
  photo is lit*. The `review_kind` field says which, so a rubric item can cite the right one
  and a later check can count them separately.
* **The licence is recorded, not re-verified at use.** Every row carries `licence`,
  `licence_url` and `source_url`; Openverse's own docs say a licence must be verified per
  work, which is why the fields are there on the row rather than in a shared note.
* **The counts are per category, not per brief.** A brief whose product is a *sauce* jar and
  one whose product is *honey* both draw on `food_jar`'s three. Depth per brief is a later
  question; the acceptance here was per category.
* **No Amazon property was contacted** — Openverse is the only source, and every refused row
  is in the library's own `refused` list with its reason.
